📖 Study Tips

IGCSE Chemistry exam strategies & study guides

🧪
Know the CAIE IGCSE Exam Structure

IGCSE Chemistry (0620) has three papers for Extended tier students. Understanding each paper's format is essential.

P2
Multiple Choice
40 questions · 45 min
30% of total grade
Covers full syllabus
P4
Theory (Extended)
Short answer + structured
1 hr 15 min · 50% of total
Deepest + most valuable
P6
Alt. to Practical
Written practical paper
1 hr · 20% of total
Experimental design + data
  • Paper 2 — Multiple Choice: 40 questions in 45 minutes (~67 sec per question). Tests every topic. Be fast and accurate. Use elimination strategy when unsure.
  • Paper 4 — Theory: The 50% paper. Short answer + extended structured questions. Every mark matters more here. Focus your revision on P4 topics: stoichiometry, organic chemistry, electrolysis, and energetics.
  • Paper 6 — Alternative to Practical: No lab needed, but you must know experimental procedures, graphs, data interpretation, and sources of error. Practice reading measurements and identifying variables.
  • Core vs Extended: Core students take P1 + P3 + P6 (grades C-G). Extended students take P2 + P4 + P6 (grades A*-G). Most of you are Extended.
⏱️
Master Time Management

The #1 reason students lose marks is not lack of knowledge — it's running out of time.

  • Survey First: 1 minute to scan the paper. Know what's coming.
  • P2 — Multiple Choice: ~67 seconds per question. If stuck, eliminate 2 options, mark your best guess, and move on. Circle it to revisit later.
  • P4 — Theory: 1 hr 15 min. Allocate time by mark count — roughly 1.5 min per mark. The 6-mark extended question at the end needs at least 8 minutes.
  • P6 — Practical: 1 hr. Don't rush experimental design questions — they're worth the most. Leave 5 min at the end to check graph labels, units, and sig figs.
  • Golden Rule: Do your strengths first. Build momentum, then tackle the hard ones.
Pro Tip: Many students spend 20+ min on the first P4 question and rush the 6-mark final question. Start from the back if that's where your strong topics are.
🔍
Analyse Questions Like a Pro

IGCSE Chemistry questions are interconnected — each sub-question builds on the previous one.

  • Look for Clues: Part (b) might give you the answer to part (a). Read ahead!
  • Watch the Mark Count: [2] means two distinct points, [3] means three. Don't write a paragraph when two words will do — and vice versa.
  • Command Words Matter:
    State → short answer Describe → details Explain → reason + result Suggest → apply logic Calculate → show working
  • Common Question Pattern: A typical structured question goes: Name → Define → Calculate → Explain → Apply. Master this flow.
📝
Memorise Key Vocabulary

Many marks are lost simply because students don't know the exact definition required.

  • Must-Know Terms: isotope, nucleon number, proton number, relative atomic mass, mole, Avogadro constant, empirical formula, molecular formula, electronegativity, allotrope, isotope, catalyst, activation energy, dynamic equilibrium.
  • English & Chinese: Know the terms in both languages. Many exam questions use specific English phrasing you must recognise instantly.
  • Flashcard Method: Write the term on one side, the definition on the other. Test yourself daily — 5 minutes a day beats 2 hours cramming.
  • Use the Review Notes section for vocabulary lists and definition practice.
Example: "What term describes atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons?" → Isotopes. One word, 2 marks. Know it cold.
📚
Smart Past Paper Practice

Not all past paper practice is equal. Here's how to do it effectively:

  • Phase 1 — Untimed: Do 2-3 papers with your notes open. Focus on understanding, not speed.
  • Phase 2 — Timed: Simulate exam conditions. 60 minutes, no notes, no phone.
  • Phase 3 — Mark Scheme Study: After marking, read the mark scheme word-for-word. Note the exact phrasing they expect. This is where you pick up 10-15% extra marks.
  • Error Log: Keep a notebook of every mistake. Group them by topic. Review before the next practice session.
  • Rotate Topics: Don't do 5 papers on the same topic. Mix it up — the real exam does.
Recommended: Use the Past Papers page — 127 papers from 2022 to 2026. Start with June 2023 → June 2024 → June 2025 as your core practice set.
⚠️
Avoid These Common Mistakes
  • Forgetting Units: Every calculation answer needs units. No unit = lost mark.
  • Mixing up Moles & Mass: Molar mass (g/mol) ≠ mass (g) ≠ moles (mol). Always label!
  • Balancing Equations Carelessly: Count atoms on both sides. Double-check.
  • Confusing Ionic & Covalent: Metal + non-metal = ionic. Non-metal + non-metal = covalent. Simple rule, often forgotten under pressure.
  • Skipping Sub-questions: Even if you can't do part (a), you can often still do parts (b) and (c). Never leave anything blank.
  • Over-thinking MC: Multiple choice questions are designed to be answered in ~1 minute. If your answer requires a complex calculation, you're probably on the wrong track.
