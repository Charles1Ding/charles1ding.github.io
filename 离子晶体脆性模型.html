Why ionic compounds shatter rather than bend under stress

1 Intact Lattice

Cations (+) and anions (−) alternate. Every ion is surrounded by oppositely charged neighbors — strong electrostatic attraction holds the crystal together.

2 Ions Displaced

Shear slides ion rows. At half a lattice spacing, like-charged ions line up. Repulsion now overcomes attraction.

3 Crystal Shatters

The violent repulsion cleaves the lattice along the slip plane. Ionic crystals fracture instead of deforming — they are brittle.

Key Concept: "Hard but Brittle"

Brittleness does not mean "weak." Ionic crystals are hard and strong (high melting point). "Brittle" means they fracture without plastic deformation. A small shear displaces the lattice only a tiny amount; once like charges meet, the repulsive force is so violent that the crystal snaps along the plane rather than bending.
