CO₂ — Molecular Structure

SOLID — molecules vibrate in place

Small, discrete O=C=O molecules. Heating only needs to overcome the weak intermolecular forces BETWEEN molecules — very little energy required → phase change at just −78 °C (sublimes at 1 atm; melts at −56 °C only under pressure).

The covalent C=O bonds INSIDE each molecule never break — each molecule stays intact as it melts, then boils.

Graphite — Giant Covalent Network

SOLID — covalent bonds fully intact

One giant molecule: every C atom is covalently bonded to 3 neighbours in strong 2-D layers. To melt graphite you must break millions of strong C–C covalent bonds (bond energy ~347 kJ/mol) → phase change only at ~3650 °C.

Weak forces exist only between layers — that is why graphite is soft and slippery, but it still will not melt until the bonds themselves break.

Same heat source, two fates: structure — not formula — decides the melting point. A ~3700 °C gap!
