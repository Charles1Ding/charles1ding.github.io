Diamond — Giant Covalent Network

SOLID — covalent bonds fully intact

One giant molecule: every C atom is covalently bonded to 4 other C atoms in a rigid 3-D tetrahedral network. All 4 valence electrons are locked in bonds — none are free to move, so diamond does not conduct. Melting means breaking millions of strong C–C bonds → only at 3550 °C. Push it sideways and nothing moves — the hardest natural substance.

Graphite — Giant Covalent Network in Layers

SOLID — covalent bonds fully intact

Every C atom is covalently bonded to 3 other C atoms in flat hexagonal layers. The 4th electron is delocalised — free to move along the layers → graphite conducts electricity. Melting still needs breaking millions of strong C–C bonds → ~3650 °C.

But layers are held to each other only by weak forces — a tiny sideways force makes them slide (and stay slid). That is why graphite is soft, slippery, and writes — pencil "lead" is sheared-off graphite layers.

Both are giant covalent networks of the SAME element — both need extreme heat to break their strong covalent bonds. But the layered structure of graphite gives it properties diamond could never have.
