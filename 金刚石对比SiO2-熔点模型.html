Diamond — Giant Covalent Network

SOLID — covalent bonds fully intact

One giant molecule: every C atom is covalently bonded to 4 other C atoms in a rigid 3-D tetrahedral network. Melting means breaking millions of strong C–C bonds (~347 kJ/mol each) → only possible around 3550 °C. The hardest natural substance.

Silicon Dioxide (SiO₂) — Giant Covalent Network

SOLID — covalent bonds fully intact

Also a giant covalent network: every Si atom bonds to 4 O atoms, and every O atom bridges 2 Si atoms (so the ratio is Si : O = 1 : 2 → formula SiO₂, not SiO₄). Breaking the whole Si–O network needs ~1710 °C. Quartz and sand are both SiO₂.

Two giant covalent networks — both need extreme heat to break their strong covalent bonds. Same class of structure, two very different (but both enormous) melting points.

C–C bond · diamond

NONPOLAR covalent

Bond length: 154 pm (sp³ C–C single bond).
ΔEN = 0 (C 2.55 − C 2.55): the shared electron pair is split equally between two identical carbons. No dipole.

Si–O bond · SiO₂

POLAR covalent

Bond length: 161 pm.
ΔEN = 1.54 (O 3.44 − Si 1.90): the shared pair is dragged toward O, so O carries δ− and Si δ+. A clear dipole.

Both are strong covalent bonds — but C–C shares its electrons equally (nonpolar), while Si–O drags its shared pair toward oxygen (polar). Same family of bond, two very different electron-sharing stories.
